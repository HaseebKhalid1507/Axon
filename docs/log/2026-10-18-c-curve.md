# The C curve: batched-decode step cost at nb 1–64, and the gemm2 partial-tile fix  (PR #81)

- Date: 2026-10-18. Branch: feat/c-curve. Base: main a032773. Status: open
- Pick / design: r12-perf-pick.md, Pick 1 ("the C curve"). The metric is aggregate tok/s per GPU at concurrency.

## What changed
- `pool_step_cost` (slots.rs tests, `#[ignore]`) is now the C-curve harness. It runs nb ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64}
  at depth 600 and 4096, with graphs on (as serve uses), median of 5 × 40 steps, and prints ms/step and × the decode
  step. It also profiles per kernel at nb 1/8/16/32/64 (an event at every `on(name)` hook of the pass, summed over the
  layers) and sweeps gemvm's batch limit (`AXON_STEP_VM`). Env: `AXON_STEP_MODEL`, `AXON_STEP_DEPTHS`, `AXON_STEP_NB`,
  `AXON_STEP_PROF`.
- **gemm2 (prefill.cu `g2_copy`)**: a tile column past the batch (nb not a multiple of 64) loads nothing. cp.async with a
  source size of 0 zero-fills it (`cp16q` / `cp4q`). Before, every such column loaded token nb − 1. Columns past nb are
  never stored, so no output bit can change. This is the fix that matters (see Results). An intermediate version loaded
  token `z mod nb` instead. The review asked for zero-fill. Both were measured, and zero-fill won at every nb 17–64 on
  four models (below).
- **Q6_K 128-token tiles only when nb > 64** (`bgemm`'s `big`). Below that, a 128-wide tile is more than half padding.
  The lm_head of a pool's 32 rows ran 2× slower in it.
- **The head of a pool step is one product over all rows** (`pass_at`, when `logits_rows`) instead of one per 16
  rows. The lm_head (7B: 447 MB of Q6_K) is now read once rather than nb/16 times. samp_tail still runs 16 rows per
  launch (its scratch is sized for that).
- **gemvm32** (NF 4). Routing is per product in `CudaQwen2::takes_gemvm(rows, nb)`. Up to 16 tokens always use gemvm.
  From 17 to 32 tokens, a product uses gemvm32 only when gemm2's 64-row tiles would number fewer than a quarter of the
  SMs (the GPU mostly idle). On the 7B that is k / v (512 rows); on the 0.5B q / k / v / o / down. `AXON_GEMVM_MAX`: 16 =
  main's routing, 32 = gemvm32 for every product up to 32 tokens, 0 / unset = by shape. gemvm64 was built, measured and
  dropped (below).
- Tests: every bit test of the product kernels now includes gemvm32 (`gpu_products_match_reference_bits`,
  `gpu_batched_products_match_gemv_bits` at nb 31/33, the swiglu epilogue); `cuda_gemm_bench` covers it too. New
  `pool_steps_past_gemvm16_match_solo`: pool steps at nb 17 / 33 / 48 / 64 under all three routings, with mixed greedy
  and sampled rows, on the 0.5B and Llama 1B, == each request alone. It exercises the one-launch head and samp_tail's
  16-row launches.
- `pool_step_cost` also takes several models (`AXON_STEP_MODEL=a,b`) and kernel builds (`AXON_STEP_KM=plain,DEFINE+…`)
  in one job.

## Why
Axon had no decode-only step cost at nb > 1. `pool_step_cost` had never been run with its output recorded. The spec
estimated, from #38's closed-loop numbers, that the nb=16 step costs 1.8× the solo step where bandwidth says 1.1×.
The curve (Phase 1, below) shows where that time goes. nb ≤ 16 is moderate: 1.59× at depth 600. The large term is
**a cliff from nb 17 to 64**, where the products leave gemvm for gemm2's 64-token tiles. The 7B step jumps from
8.6 ms at nb 16 to 13.4 ms at nb 24, and stays flat at ~13.5 ms up to 64. By kernel, the fused gate+up alone goes
from 3.5 ms (gemvm16, gate + up) to 5.5 ms at nb 32, yet only 2.9 ms at nb 64. That is the same grid with half the
real columns, and it runs 2× slower.

`cuda_gemm_bench` (RTX 4090, synthetic 7B shapes) confirms it in isolation: gemm2s on the 3584 × 18944 Q4_K gate takes
142 µs at nb 1, 96 µs at nb 32 and 56 µs at nb 64. The fewer real tokens in the tile, the slower it runs. The
activations of all padding columns came from one address (`min(z, nb − 1)`). Spreading them over the real tokens
(`z mod nb`) removed most of the pathology: gate_up at nb 32 went from 5.49 to 3.05 ms. Not loading them at all
(zero-fill) is faster still (7B step at nb 32: 10.37 → 9.81 ms). So the padding loads were the cost in themselves, not
just the shared address. Why one shared row was the worst case stays UNVERIFIED (my guess: L2 same-sector pressure
in the cp.async stream).

What the curve says:
1. **nb 17–64 GEMMs** (gemm2 on partial tiles) was the top term at serving depth. Fixed here.
2. **The head** re-read the lm_head once per 16 rows: 2.6 ms at nb 64. Fixed here (0.69 ms).
3. **Attention at depth is bandwidth, not overhead.** At 4096 it is 18.6 ms of the 31.8 ms nb=64 step. 64 rows × 4096
   positions × 57 KB = 15 GB, i.e. ~15 ms at 1008 GB/s, so it is already at ~80 % of DRAM bandwidth. Kernel work can't
   shrink that much; only fewer bytes can (q8 KV, already a flag; paged prefix sharing).
4. **The pool's nb=1 step is 1.20× the decode step** (6.49 vs 5.42 ms): separate q/k/v GEMMs, gemvm8 at ~700 GB/s vs the
   decode GEMV's ~930. Serve doesn't pay this at C=1 (a lone request takes the single-stream path, #38), but every
   batched step does. This is the next lead for nb ≤ 16.
5. 0.5B is launch- and latency-bound throughout (1.9× solo already at nb 1).

Spec hypothesis check: nb=16 is 1.59× (depth 600), not 1.8×. The 1.8× estimate folded the nb 17–32 cliff and
prefill into one number. The gap to the 1.1× ideal at nb 16 is still ~2.6 ms, above the kill rule's 1 ms, so this was
a GO.

## What didn't work
- **gemvm32 / gemvm64 for every product.** This was the spec's candidate (a): push gemvm past 16 tokens so the weights
  are still read once.
  - 7B step at nb 32: 18.5 ms with gemvm32 vs 14.7 with gemm2. At nb 64: 37.2 ms with gemvm64 vs 12.7.
  - `cuda_gemm_bench`, gate 3584 × 18944: gemvm32 138 µs vs gemm2s 96 µs at nb 32; gemvm64 282 µs vs 56 µs at nb 64.
  - Why: gemvm's CTA is 16 rows, and each CTA streams all its tokens' activations from L2. At 32 tokens that is K bytes
    per token vs 16 rows × 0.56 K bytes of weights, 3.5× more activation than weight traffic. gemvm64 also spills
    (255 regs, 80 B local).
  - Outcome: gemvm32 is kept only for products too small for gemm2's tiles to fill the GPU (`takes_gemvm`); gemvm64 is
    removed.
  - What would make gemvm wider work: more rows per CTA sharing activations through shared memory, i.e. gemm2s with a
    16- or 32-token tile.
- **z mod nb** for the padding columns: superseded by zero-fill, which is faster everywhere (table above).
- **The head in one product without the tile fix** made nb 32 slower (14.7 vs 14.1 ms). The 152k-row head took gemm2's
  128-wide Q6_K tile. Hence `big` only past 64 tokens.
- Not tried (next leads, by size): the nb ≤ 16 GEMV efficiency (gemvm8/16 at 540–800 GB/s vs the decode GEMV's ~930,
  and q/k/v as three launches); one attention launch for all rows (attn_blk_b launches per 16 queries, 1.6 → 3.2 ms
  at nb 32 → 64 at depth 600, where bandwidth predicts ~2.2 ms at 64); a 32-token gemm2s tile.
