# Multi-query block attention (attn_blk_mq) for few queries at depth  (PR #TBD)

- Date: 2026-10-18. Branch: feat/attn-mq, stacked on feat/paged-kv-pool (#71) at ce48d6d (its merge of main cf8dee4).
  Status: open.
- Pick / design: r12-perf-pick.md §4 (pick 3). The spec's kill rule fired for its headline case (128 queries); the
  foreman kept the track for ≤ 32 queries (chunk 32 at depth, draft verify rows). See "Kill rule" below.

## What changed
- `decode.cu`: `attn_mq_t`, plus `attn_blk_mq_{64,128}` and the q8 / q8g32 twins. One CTA takes a kv head, 64-position
  blocks `blockIdx.y + k·gridDim.y`, and 16 consecutive queries of one sequence. It has `group` warps, and each warp
  takes the m16 tile of 16 rows (query, head) = 16 / group queries × group heads. The CTA loads each K / V tile
  once for all 16 queries, through the page table (`att_rb`). Per row, steps 1-8 of the canonical order are
  attn_blk_t's one-warp path (`att_qfrag`, `att_scores`, `att_softmax`, `att_pv`). It writes the partial
  (o_b, m_b, l_b) of every block with 64b < np exactly where attn_blk_b puts it, and the existing `attn_cmb_b`
  folds them. P·V runs 64 dims at a time (128 registers at `__launch_bounds__(256, 2)`; 12 B of spill on sm_89).
- `batch.rs`: `att_flash(nb)` replaces `nb > att_per_query(hd)`. A one-sequence pass of up to `MQ_MAX` = 32 queries
  (hd 128; hd 64 keeps 48) takes the per-query path, which is now attn_blk_mq + attn_cmb_b, att_per_query queries
  per launch (partials buffer unchanged). Larger passes keep attn_fa_b, as before. Multi-sequence rows (serve
  decode) keep attn_blk_b. `AXON_ATT_MQ=0` restores attn_blk_b for ≤ 16 queries and attn_fa_b above that.
  `AXON_ATT_MQ_MAX=N` moves the crossover.
- Tests: `gpu_batched_attention_matches_decode_bits` checks attn_blk_mq's partials and act against decode (all 7
  layouts, q8 included, z0 > 0, partial last CTA, NaN past the batch). `gpu_paged_split_and_batched_bits` runs it
  through page tables. `prefill_bits` gets random chunk sizes of 1-48 and a [24, 32, 31, 2, 47] cycle.
  `cuda_attn_bench` gains attn_blk_mq and the two per-query paths over all queries. `cuda_attn_mq_sweep` is new
  (no environment needed, so it runs on pods).

## Bits
A row's mma result doesn't depend on its tile-mates, so each partial has attn_blk_b's bits. The one difference is
prefill's documented asymmetry (decode.cu "Tensor-core attention", end): the tile holds positions up to the CTA's
last query, so an earlier query sees real V rows where attn_blk_b has zero fill, with p = +0 there. At most this
flips the sign of an exactly-zero o_b[d]; the fold can't turn that into anything but a zero, and quant_warp erases
it. A NaN value written by the batch poisons its earlier queries here too, as in attn_fa_b.

## Kill rule (r12-perf-pick §4): FAILED as written
"Kill if the 20-minute bench shows blk_mq is not ≥ 1.8× attn_fa_b at 128 queries / 24k." MEASURED on an RTX 4090
(1018-043837-1ff9): mq + attn_cmb_b over 128 queries at 24k = 1964 us vs attn_fa_b 1486 us = **0.76×** (and 0.49×
attn_fa_bs, 969 us, the variant production picks there). The chunk-128 admission case is dead for this design.

### Why 128 lost: the partials
attn_blk_b's contract is one f32 partial per (query, head, block): o_b is hd floats = 512 B (hd 128) written by the
attention kernel and read back by attn_cmb_b, 1 KB of traffic per row-block. With 16 queries × 7 heads sharing a
tile, the KV a row-block costs is 2 × 64 × 128 × 2 B / 112 = 292 B. attn_blk_mq removes the per-query KV re-read
(÷ 16), but the partials stay ~3.5× the KV it still reads. At 128 queries / 24k that is 128 × 28 × 384 × 1 KB =
1.4 GB per layer (ESTIMATED), against ~0.4 GB of KV for 8 walks. attn_fa_b folds in registers and writes only y.
MEASURED split on the 4090 at 24k: for 32 queries, mq 284 us + cmb 209 us; for 64 queries in one launch, mq 625 us
+ cmb 409 us. The fold is about 40 % of the time, and the attention kernel itself is mostly partial stores. The
spec's arithmetic (5.5 GB / 5.5 ms for chunk 128) counted the KV and left the partials out.

### Idea (ESTIMATED, not built): no partials, the fold in registers across a CTA's blocks
The canonical fold is sequential in ascending block order from (−inf, 0, 0), so a CTA can only fold the blocks it
owns in registers if it owns a row's blocks 0..nblk−1 in order. That is attn_fa_b's shape, latency-bound with few
rows. A version that keeps the bits: chain the CTAs of one row range. CTA k computes (o_b, m_b, l_b) for its
contiguous block range in parallel with everyone, keeps them in registers or shared memory, then waits on a flag
for CTA k−1's running state (M, L, O[hd]: ~0.5 KB per row). It folds its own blocks in ascending order with
att_fold_w / att_fold_o, publishes, and sets its flag. This is decoupled-lookback-style ordering: a fixed order, no
atomics in any reduction, the same fold sequence as attn_cmb, so the same bits. Traffic per row drops from 1 KB per
block to ~1 KB per CTA boundary (e.g. 8 per row instead of 384 at 24k). The serial part is the chain of
handoffs: ~8 × (fold of 48 blocks ≈ 2-3 us + flag latency ≈ 1-2 us) ≈ 30-40 us per layer, against today's attn_cmb_b
at 409 us for 64 queries. The risk is forward progress (CTA k spinning before k−1 is resident), handled as CUB
does, by taking the block order from an atomic ticket rather than blockIdx. It needs its own design and critic
round.
