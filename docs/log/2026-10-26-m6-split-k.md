# M6: fixed-chunk two-level K chain + split-K for the narrow products  (NO-GO, PR #148 closed)

- Date: 2026-10-26. Branch: `perf/m6-split-k` (head 323bebc, kept, not merged). Base: main ae1e26b.
  Status: **rejected by its pre-registered kill rule** (owner call 10-26).
- Pick / design: roadmap item 2, step 1. The H100 anatomy (#125, [log](2026-10-26-h100-anatomy-results.md))
  put the narrow products (q, k, v, wo, down) at 6.3 ms of the 7B nb = 128 decode-pool step. At nb ≤ 128 they
  run gemm2s with 56 (q/wo/down) or 16 (k/v) tiles on 132 SMs, so they are occupancy-bound, and splitting K
  addresses that.

## What changed (on the branch)
- **NUMERICS_VERSION 1 → 2.** Every GPU product sums K as a two-level chain: the version-1 fma chain within
  each fixed chunk, then the chunk partials in index order. The chunk depends on the shape only: 512 values
  when the matrix has ≤ 1,024 rows, else 1,024. So decode == prefill == verify and the cross-GPU bits hold.
  - Kernels: every product kernel in decode.cu, gemma.cu, prefill.cu and hopper.cu, plus `gemv_ref`.
- **Split-K:** gemm2s runs one chunk per `blockIdx.z` for 16 < nb ≤ 128, plus a `gemm_kcombine` pass, when
  there are fewer 64×64 tiles than SMs.
- **Large-nb kernels:** they fold the chunks in-block, keeping the chunk totals in local memory. Kept in
  registers, they made the hot loops spill.
- `AXON_KCHUNK=512|1024|2048|65536` (test/kit only; part of the cache key and `numerics_tag`) forces one
  chunk. 65536 reproduces version 1.

## Pre-registered kill rule (PR #148 body, written before any timing)
- H100 7B graphed nb = 128 step (`pool_step_cost`, depth 600) −10 % vs main;
- H100 tg128 not worse;
- no regression > 2 % on bella and a 4090 (tg128, pp512, pp2100; Qwen2.5 0.5B and 7B).

## Why (ESTIMATED, not profiled)
- Splitting K can only win where the narrow products are occupancy-bound.
- The two-level order is paid by **every** product kernel:
  - the extra chunk accumulator;
  - the per-chunk flush of totals to local memory;
  - new spills in gemvm32 Q4_K/Q5_K (sm_89 / sm_90a), gemmh2 (36 B, sm_90a) and gemm2t_gu (8 B, sm_120).
- Prefill (gemm2t at large nb) lost 14–27 %. The nb = 128 pool step, which runs the gate/up/down products at
  large tiles too, lost 14 %, more than split-K saved on q/k/v/wo.
- A per-kernel profile wasn't taken (the A/B and the step measurement were enough for the rule).

**A restructured test** (branch commit 323bebc; the same patch applied to main passes):
- **Grading:** against the f64 reference instead of the CPU, with c = CPU, w = CPU per-row kernels and
  v1 = main's GPU numbers (pinned):
  - top-1 ≥ min(v1, c, w) − max(2, |c − w|);
  - mean KL ≤ max(v1, c, w) × (larger/smaller of c and w);
  - max KL ≤ max(v1, c, w) × (the same ratio, on the max KLs).
- **GPU vs CPU** is kept only as loose sanity bars.
- **Results:**
  - Main passes (avante 1026-200744-1d88): Q8_0 194 / 0.0093 / 0.633 against bars 191 / 0.0183 / 0.678;
    Q4_K_M 191 / 0.0201 / 0.863 against 189 / 0.0244 / 0.972.
  - M6 passes (bella 1026-200752-7d70).
  - The c2048 variant's Q4_K_M row (188, max 1.034) would fail.
- **Caveat:** the bars were **not written blind**. M6's numbers were already known from the bisection when the
  rule was chosen, though the bars use only main's and the CPU's values.
- **Earlier attempts:** commits ad16b67, 98c15d9 and a7a4ebb exempted asserts one at a time (reference-justified
  flips, the max |dlogit| bar, continuation margins). That was rightly stopped as fitting the test to the change.
- **Status:** worth proposing on its own merits, with bars calibrated blind on a future numerics change.

## What didn't work / what would settle a retry
- The fixed-chunk order costs the large-nb kernels far more than split-K saves on the narrow ones (4090).
- A retry (M6b) would have to make the chunked order nearly free in gemm2t / gemvm / gemmh2 before any split.
  - Options: chunk totals flushed to shared memory at chunk boundaries, or the chunk equal to the kernel's own
    K-tile.
  - Pre-register the large-nb kernel cost (≤ 2 %) as the first gate, measured on a 4090 before an H100 is booked.
- Alternatively, split-K in a way that doesn't change the order: none is known under the bit contract.
